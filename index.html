ILine · 3-Wheeler · Noida · Pricing brief

Strategy B — Price to a Daily Earnings Target

How the 3-wheeler tariff is set so an average vehicle clears its daily earnings target — and why the fix is a small evening surge, not a price hike.

Basis: real completed orders iline_riderequests_v2 Noida · 3W · last 15 days all-day

01 Objective

Set the tariff so an average vehicle clears a target daily earning (₹1,500/day) at its breakeven order rate — then decide whether pricing should move up or down.

02 The core identity

Daily earning / vehicle = Real AOV × OPD Target: ₹1,500/day at breakeven OPD = 3 → required AOV = ₹500 / order

AOV = average order value = average grossFare per completed order.  OPD = orders per vehicle per day (breakeven assumption = 3).

03 Decision rule

AOV × OPD > Targetsurplus → room to decrease price (be more competitive)
AOV × OPD < Targetshortfall → increase price, or add surge
AOV × OPD ≈ Targetalready priced at the floor

04 AOV decomposition

AOV = avg trip distance (km) × effective ₹ per km

Effective ₹/km = AOV / avg_distance. It is not a tariff slab rate — it is the blended realised rate, and it falls with distance because the fixed base fare is amortised over more kilometres (short trips carry a high ₹/km, long trips a low one). To lift AOV at constant distance, raise the effective rate — equivalently, nudge the base fare, which lifts every order equally.

07 The evening-surge lever

ILine currently runs ~0% evening surge — at the same distance bucket, evening AOV ≈ morning AOV (within ±3%). The lower overall evening AOV is a distance-mix effect (evening trips are shorter), not a pricing one.

The Strategy-B lever is an evening surge on orders after 3 PM (~42% of orders), which lifts all-day AOV without touching morning pricing:

all-day AOV(surge) = [ Σ(morning_orders × morning_AOV) + Σ(evening_orders × evening_AOV × (1 + surge)) ] / total_orders surge to hit target = ( Target/OPD × total − morning_contrib ) / evening_contrib₀ − 1
Competitors already run ~17% evening surge, so there is large headroom.

08 Discount layer

The discount finding. ILine funds a ~22% promo on ~100% of rides (avg ₹109 on ₹492 gross). The driver still earns the full gross, so the vehicle clears ~₹1,476/day — but ILine's net realization is only ~₹1,149/day, ₹351 under target. And 94.5% of rides are retained customers discounted ~22% — the biggest lever is trimming the retained discount, not acquisition.
Ride discount = before 1 PM: max(segment %, morning %) · 1 PM onward: segment % # higher wins, never stacked Blended disc % = 36.5% (before 1 PM) · [ s_new·max(d_new,d_morn) + s_ret·max(d_ret,d_morn) ] + 63.5% (1 PM+) · [ s_new·d_new + s_ret·d_ret ] Net AOV = Gross AOV × (1 − blended discount %) Gross ₹/day = Gross AOV × OPD # driver earning Net ₹/day = Net AOV × OPD # ILine realization

09 Result

Where ILine stands today

  • Real AOV ≈ ₹493/order → ₹493 × 3 = ₹1,479/day → ₹21 short of ₹1,500.
  • Decomposition: ₹493 = 13.5 km × ₹36.5/km; target ₹500 needs +₹0.5/km (≈ +₹7 base fare).
  • A cost tariff re-solved to hit ₹1,500 under the real mix lands ≈ ILine's current tariff — current pricing is already at the earnings floor.
  • The evening surge closes the gap on its own: a ~3.8% surge (or flat +₹17/evening order) → ₹1,500/day, with no change to morning pricing and still below competitors' evening fares.

Conclusion. Strategy B does not call for a morning price increase. It calls for introducing a modest evening surge (currently zero) on the ~42% of orders after 3 PM. A conservative 5–10% surge clears ₹1,500/day comfortably while keeping ILine the cheapest player in the evening.

10 Open items

  • Tighten the "evening" window from "after 3 PM" to a true peak band (e.g. 6–9 PM) — fewer orders would carry the surge, so the surge % to hit target would rise.
  • Confirm whether ₹1,500 is fare revenue (GMV) or driver take-home net of commission — if the latter, divide the target by (1 − commission) first.
  • Strategy A (match the market) to be documented separately.
